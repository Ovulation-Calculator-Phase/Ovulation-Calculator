Trusted sources

Ovulation and fertility resources

Use these official health-information sources to learn more about menstrual cycles, ovulation, pregnancy, and when to seek professional advice.

Professional guidance

American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists

ACOG provides patient education about women’s health, menstrual cycles, reproductive health, pregnancy, and symptoms that may need clinical attention.

Visit ACOG Women’s Health

Public health service

NHS

The UK National Health Service offers accessible information about periods, symptoms, pregnancy, fertility, and situations where medical advice is appropriate.

Read NHS period guidance

Plain-language reference

MedlinePlus

MedlinePlus, from the U.S. National Library of Medicine, explains menstruation and related health terms in clear language for patients and families.

Read MedlinePlus menstruation

How to use sources

Read with context

Official sources can explain general health information, but they cannot diagnose an individual situation. Bring persistent symptoms, unusual bleeding, severe pain, fertility concerns, or pregnancy questions to a qualified healthcare professional.
