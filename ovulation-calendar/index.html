Calendar guide

How to read an ovulation calendar

An ovulation calendar turns cycle information into a planning estimate. This guide explains what each marker means and where calendar predictions have limits.

Create your personal calendar

What the phases mean

Menstrual phase: the period begins on the first day of bleeding. Follicular phase: the body prepares for ovulation and length can vary. Fertile window: the several days before estimated ovulation and the day after. Ovulation: the calendar's estimated single day. Luteal phase: the time between ovulation and the next expected period.

Use the calendar as a record

Print the dates, note symptoms or LH-test results, and compare estimates with what your body does over time. A pattern can be useful context for a clinician, while a single calendar prediction cannot confirm ovulation.

Know the limits

Illness, stress, postpartum changes, breastfeeding, PCOS, perimenopause, and hormonal contraception can shift timing. Calendar estimates are not contraception and do not guarantee conception. Read our guidance for irregular periods and ovulation symptoms, or review how the calculation works.
